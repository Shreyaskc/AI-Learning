Chapter 4: Optimization: how learning changes parameters. Curvature and conditioning.

A convex function lies below the line joining any two points on its graph. For a differentiable convex objective, a stationary point is a global minimum. Linear least squares is convex in its coefficients; a deep network's objective generally is not. The Hessian contains second partial derivatives and describes local curvature. A narrow valley with very different curvature across directions is ill-conditioned: one learning rate may oscillate in one direction and crawl in another. Scaling inputs can help; it does not eliminate every source of poor conditioning.

The optimizer names in this paragraph are an optional orientation. Momentum smooths gradient history: Velocity at step t equals beta times the previous velocity plus one minus beta times the current gradient, then step along v sub t. Adam keeps running averages of gradients and squared gradients. It corrects the early averages for their initialization and scales each coordinate by a stabilized square root of its squared-gradient average. These averages are also called first and second moments. It adapts coordinate step scales. AdamW decouples weight decay from the adaptive gradient calculation. These are optimization procedures, not substitutes for validation. Scheduler, batch size, precision and initialization can all alter behavior.
