Question 1. Explain why duplicating a feature can hurt naive Bayes confidence. Pause now and try your answer. When you are ready, continue. Solution: the conditional independence model can count the same evidence twice, making likelihood ratios too extreme.

Question 2. A tree performs well on delays 0 to 10 days; will it extrapolate linearly to 100 minus day delays? Pause now and try your answer. When you are ready, continue. Solution: ordinary regression trees predict leaf averages, so they do not naturally extrapolate a trend.

Question 3. A model beats a baseline by zero point two% after 1,000 trials. What next? Pause now and try your answer. When you are ready, continue. Solution: account for selection and uncertainty, use a preserved evaluation set, inspect slices and compare value and complexity before adoption.

Exercises: (1) Change the label to feature x one is greater than zero and define new splits before running. Predict which model should improve. Pause to try this exercise. Then continue for the solution: a linear score and shallow tree can represent this boundary; complexity is no longer required, although finite data and noise still matter. (2) Increase maximum depth until training fit approaches perfection. What evidence would justify retaining it? Pause to try this exercise. Then continue for the solution: validation improvement and useful error behavior under an unchanged protocol, not training accuracy. (3) Remove scaling for all models. Pause to try this exercise. Then continue for the solution: trees are largely invariant to monotonic per-feature rescaling when thresholds transform correspondingly; gradient optimization is sensitive to scales. Compare convergence and loss with controlled training budgets rather than assuming every model changes identically.
