00

Start here: learn, build, explain

New to AI? [Start with the first lesson](01-foundations.md), where a support-ticket example introduces a learned prediction. You do not need to pass a quiz or install anything first.

01

What AI systems learn

Prerequisite: basic programming. Outcomes: distinguish AI terms, frame a learning problem, and make a split before fitting anything.

02

Vectors, matrices, logs and derivatives

Prerequisite: 01. Outcomes: track dimensions, compute similarity, interpret logarithms, and apply the chain rule. These tools describe computations; notation should expose mistakes rather than hide them.

03

Probability, statistics and uncertainty

Prerequisites: 02, especially logs. Outcomes: reason with base rates, distinguish distributions from samples, and interpret uncertainty without turning correlation into causation.

04

Optimization: how learning changes parameters

Prerequisites: 02–03. Outcomes: derive a training update, diagnose optimization versus generalization failure, and understand regularization.

05

Classical machine learning and useful baselines

Prerequisites: 01–04. Outcomes: select a model family from assumptions, fit a simple classifier, and recognize when a neural network is unnecessary.

06

Evaluation, leakage and decision quality

Prerequisites: 01, 03 and 05. Outcomes: select metrics from actions, quantify uncertainty, detect contamination, and define abstention.

07

Neural networks and representation learning

Prerequisites: 02–06. Outcomes: trace forward and backward passes, distinguish architectures, and understand why learned representations help.

08

Tokens, embeddings and transformers

Prerequisites: 02–03 and 07. Outcomes: trace text into next-token logits, calculate masked attention, and distinguish architecture from training objective.

09

LLM pretraining, adaptation and alignment

Prerequisites: 04, 06 and 08. Outcomes: separate training stages, compute language-model loss, and reason about adaptation and reward limitations.

10

Retrieval, ranking and grounded generation

Prerequisites: 02, 06, 08–09. Outcomes: implement lexical retrieval, separate ranking from generation quality, and design authorized evidence assembly.

11

Workflows, tools, context, memory and graphs

Prerequisites: 06 and 10; revisit 16 for threat modeling before private retrieval or side effects. Outcomes: implement bounded control flow, reason about retries, and separate stored context from learned weights.

12

Multimodal and generative modeling

Prerequisites: 03, 07–09. Outcomes: distinguish alignment from generation, calculate a contrastive example, and identify modality-specific failures. This chapter provides orientation and toy calculations, not full vision/audio training competence.

13

Data and feature platforms

Prerequisites: 01, 06 and 10–11. Outcomes: construct historical features without future knowledge, design feature/index lifecycles, and reason about training-serving consistency.

14

Training compute, hardware and inference serving

Prerequisites: 07–09 and 13. Outcomes: estimate memory and capacity with explicit assumptions, separate prefill from decode, and reason about parallelism and efficiency. Numeric estimates here are hypothetical arithmetic, not hardware benchmarks.

15

MLOps, observability and release discipline

Prerequisites: 06, 10–14. Outcomes: version a complete AI behavior, define actionable monitors, and plan rollout and recovery.

16

Security, privacy and responsible AI

Prerequisites: 06 and 10–15. Outcomes: identify trust boundaries, test concrete controls, and reason about harm beyond aggregate accuracy. This is engineering guidance, not legal advice or a claim of compliance with any jurisdiction.

17

Experiments and reading research

Prerequisites: 03 and 06; use 09 or 14 for domain-specific studies. Outcomes: turn a claim into a falsifiable experiment and distinguish results, explanation and speculation.

18

Domain electives: time, recommendations and anomalies

Prerequisites: 03, 05–06 and 13. Outcomes: avoid common domain-specific leakage, compute a baseline ranking/forecast, and define when a task needs deeper specialization. These are worked orientations, not complete courses in causal inference, forecasting or reinforcement learning.

19

Projects, mastery gates and interview practice

Prerequisites: the relevant core chapters; 06 and 16 are mandatory for every project. Outcomes: produce inspectable evidence, defend a design, and communicate uncertainty. Reading completion is not demonstrated mastery.
