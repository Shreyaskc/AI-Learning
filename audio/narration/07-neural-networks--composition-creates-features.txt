Chapter 7: Neural networks and representation learning. Composition creates features.

Each hidden unit first makes a weighted score, then applies a nonlinear function to that score. For x equals (2,1), rows (1,negative 1) and (zero point five,zero point five) with zero biases give scores 1 and 1.5. rectified linear unit keeps these positive scores, giving hidden outputs (1,1.5).

The compact layer notation is Hidden representation h equals activation function phi applied to W times x plus b: W contains one weight row per output unit, b its bias, phi the activation function and h the resulting hidden vector. W is output-width by input-width under column-vector convention; b has output width. phi is a nonlinear activation. With no nonlinearity, stacking affine layers collapses into one affine map: Applying two affine layers without a nonlinear activation gives a single affine layer: the combined weight is W two times W one, and the combined bias is W two times b one plus b two. Depth alone would not create a more expressive nonlinear boundary.

rectified linear unit is max(0,z); it keeps positive values and blocks negatives. Its derivative is 1 for z greater than 0 and 0 for z less than 0; software chooses a convention at zero, where the derivative is not unique. Sigmoid saturates for large positive or negative scores; repeatedly multiplying small derivatives can make early-layer gradients tiny. Residual connections add an identity path, letting layers learn a correction rather than the entire transformation.

Some universal-approximation results say a sufficiently capable network can represent broad classes of functions under stated conditions. They do not show that finite data and an optimizer will find that representation or generalize.

Representation learning trains intermediate features that make an objective easier. These features can transfer to other tasks if useful structure overlaps, but can also encode spurious shortcuts. A picture classifier may learn a background watermark rather than the object. Training loss alone cannot distinguish the intended mechanism.
