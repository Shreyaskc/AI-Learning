Chapter 7: Neural networks and representation learning. A complete backprop calculation.

Take input x equal to two. The hidden activation h is the rectified linear unit of weight w one times x plus bias b one. The output z is weight w two times h plus bias b two. The loss L is z minus target y, squared, divided by two. Choose weight w one equal to zero point five, bias b one equal to zero, weight w two equal to three, bias b two equal to zero, and target y equal to four. In the forward pass, hidden preactivation is one, h is one, z is three, and loss is zero point five. In the backward pass, the derivative of loss with respect to z is negative one. The derivative of loss with respect to weight w two is negative one times h, which is negative one. The derivative for output bias b two is negative one. Through the hidden layer, the derivative of loss with respect to h is negative one times three, which is negative three. Since preactivation is positive, the rectified linear unit derivative is one. The derivative for weight w one is negative three times one times input x, which is negative six. The derivative for hidden bias b one is negative three. Every gradient follows local chain-rule multiplication.

A computation graph stores operations and dependencies. Reverse-mode automatic differentiation accumulates derivatives from outputs back to parameters, efficiently producing many parameter gradients for a scalar loss. It is neither symbolic algebra for arbitrary expressions nor finite differences. Gradients must be zeroed or intentionally accumulated between updates in frameworks that accumulate them. Training mode and evaluation mode affect operations such as dropout; disabling gradients alone is not the same thing as switching mode.

Run learning.py, which checks this two-layer gradient by finite differences. Compare a hand derivative to the numerical approximation rather than trusting either implementation blindly.
