00 · Start here: learn, build, explain

New to AI? Start with the first lesson, where a support-ticket example introduces a learned prediction. You do not need to pass a quiz or install anything first.

Already familiar with some ideas? Use the optional diagnostic below to find topics to revisit. It includes concepts taught later, so an unfamiliar question is a route into learning, not a failed entrance exam.

You can begin on your phone: read the explanation, think through the question, reveal the answer and compare the reasoning. When you have a computer, run the small lab to inspect the same mechanism in code. Browser study checkboxes and Q&A self-checks are stored separately in that browser; they do not sync across devices or certify mastery.

The destination is the ability to explain, implement, evaluate and design AI systems. Interview practice helps reveal weak reasoning; no course guarantees every interview result.

Follow the story

First define a decision and the information available for it (01). Turn examples into numbers, express uncertainty and learn from error (02–05). Check whether the result works for the real decision (06). Then understand neural/language models (07–09), build evidence and controlled actions around them (10–13), and operate/challenge the system (14–18). Use projects and changing interview scenarios to demonstrate the learning (19).

How to use the course

Read in order on your first pass. Each chapter states prerequisites and outcomes, develops a mechanism, works an example, then asks you to retrieve the idea without looking. Write your answer before opening a solution. Run the linked lab, change an assumption, and explain why the output changed. An explanation that merely repeats a formula is not yet understanding.

The fourteen offline labs include trained logistic/tree/MLP and character models, BM25/answer-evaluation boundaries, cross-process durable recovery and measured mock serving. The 29-module crosswalk explains where implementation depth stops.

The examples use invented support tickets, libraries and equipment. They contain no employer data or claimed personal achievements. Python labs require only the standard library and make no network calls. They demonstrate mechanisms on tiny data; they are not trained production models or evidence of real-world accuracy.

Three learning paths

First principles: work through 00–19, roughly 100–150 hours including exercises and projects. This is a planning estimate, not a measured completion time. Allocate more time if probability or calculus is new.

Practicing engineer: take the diagnostic below; still read 01 and 06. Review 02–05 where you cannot derive the answers, then complete 07–19 and two projects. Existing Python ability is useful but does not replace evaluation skills.

Architect interview rehearsal: after the first pass, use 06, 10, 11 and 13–19. Spend 45 minutes on a design, 15 on a capacity estimate, and 15 on a failure analysis. Ask another person to challenge assumptions. Keep a log of errors rather than memorizing polished scripts.

Optional diagnostic: choose what to review

Score one point only if you can explain the reasoning, not just name the term.

Why can fitting a scaler before the train/test split contaminate evaluation?

Answer and reasoning

test distribution statistics influence the fitted pipeline; fitting belongs within each training fold.

A rare fault has prevalence 1%; a detector has 90% sensitivity and 10% false-positive rate. Is a positive result usually correct?

Answer and reasoning

no; 90/(90+990), about 8.3% precision in 10,000 cases.

Differentiate (wx-y)^2/2 with respect to w.

Answer and reasoning

(wx-y)x; the x comes from the chain rule.

Why is 99% accuracy weak evidence on a 1% positive problem?

Answer and reasoning

predicting every case negative achieves it.

What does a causal attention mask prohibit?

Answer and reasoning

Why does adding retrieval not guarantee truth?

Answer and reasoning

retrieval may miss evidence; evidence may be false or outdated; the generator may not follow it.

What must a retried payment-like tool call carry?

Answer and reasoning

an enforced idempotency key and authorization context; a prompt instruction is insufficient.

Why is event time alone insufficient for historical feature construction?

Answer and reasoning

an event may have arrived after the historical decision.

What differs between a latency average and a p99 latency?

Answer and reasoning

mean measures overall average, p99 marks the slow-tail boundary for 99% of observed requests.

Review rhythm and dependency reference

01 defines the problem and splitting contract. 02 → 03 → 04 supplies math. Those plus 01 feed 05 → 06 → 07 → 08 → 09. Then 06+08+09 → 10 → 11; 07+08+09 → 12. 06+10+11 → 13 → 14 → 15. Chapter 16 deepens security. Chapter 17 uses 03+06 for experiments; 03+05+06+13 → 18 covers electives and 19 integrates projects/interviews. Chapter 16's threat modeling should also be revisited while building 10 and 11.

After every chapter, close the page and explain its mechanism in 90 seconds. Review the next day, one week later and one month later. On each review: define three terms, redo one calculation, solve one changed example, and name one failure mode. If you fail, reread the relevant section and schedule another next-day attempt. Spacing dates are a practical study convention, not a universal optimal schedule.

Evidence labels

Foundation means a mathematical identity or well-established mechanism under stated assumptions. Empirical means a finding on a particular dataset, model or experimental setup; it may not transfer. Design judgment means a recommendation whose merit depends on requirements. Open question means unsettled evidence. Primary-source references are collected in Sources; they are learning evidence, not a live model leaderboard.

Computer labs: when you are ready

These commands are for a terminal on a computer, opened in the downloaded repository folder. They are not needed to begin reading on a phone. The examples use synthetic data and need no paid API or GPU.

From the repository root:

python3 labs/foundations.py
python3 labs/learning.py
python3 labs/attention.py
python3 labs/retrieval.py
python3 labs/platform.py
python3 tools/run_labs.py
python3 -m unittest discover -s labs -p 'test_*.py'
python3 tools/build.py
python3 tools/check.py

Open index.html for the generated reader. Markdown in course/ is the source of truth. No GPU, paid API, external library or account is needed.
