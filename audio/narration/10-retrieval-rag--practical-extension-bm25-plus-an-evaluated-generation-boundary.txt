The request builder first filters tenant access, then computes corpus statistics and candidates within that authorized subset. It emits query, tenant, instruction and source-tagged context, plus a SHA-256 hash of the exact request. Corpus statistics can themselves reveal information, so this tiny design avoids computing them across unauthorized documents. Large systems may choose other authorized-index strategies and must analyze their leakage and performance tradeoffs.

The response schema contains abstain and a list of an object containing generated text and its sources claims. The evaluator checks: is relevant evidence in the context; is the answer withheld appropriately given that context; does every claim cite a supplied source that explicitly supports it; and does the response cover the required answer facts? Grounded-but-irrelevant text is not automatically a correct answer. The exact-match fact checklist is intentionally small and curated. It will misgrade valid paraphrases and does not replace human semantic evaluation.

The seven-case fixture suite includes one successful reset answer, an answer containing one supported and one fabricated claim, two correct abstentions, a paraphrase retrieval miss, a wrong citation, and an instruction-like malicious-source case. Recorded results show one retrieval miss, 2 divided by 5 supported claims and 3 divided by 7 end-to-end successes. These counts were injected to verify evaluation failure paths. They are evidence that the harness distinguishes these conditions, not evidence that a language model resists injection or answers at that accuracy.
