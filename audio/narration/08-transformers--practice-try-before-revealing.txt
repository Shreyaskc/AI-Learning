Question 1. With T equals 10,d sub k equals 4,d sub v equals 6, what are shapes of S and A times V? Pause now and try your answer. When you are ready, continue. Solution: 10 by 10 and 10 by 6.

Question 2. Why can teacher forcing train positions together while decoding is sequential? Pause now and try your answer. When you are ready, continue. Solution: training already knows target prefixes; inference must obtain each newly generated prefix.

Question 3. Does lower temperature solve hallucinations? Pause now and try your answer. When you are ready, continue. Solution: no; it can make a wrong high-probability answer more repeatable.

Question 1. Change X at position 1 from (0,1) to (4,negative 3), recompute Q,K,V and the entire block. Which previous outputs may change? Pause now and try your answer. When you are ready, continue. Solution: position zero cannot change under the causal mask because all permitted dependencies originate at position zero; position 1 can change. Test all output coordinates, not just row-weight sums. Without the mask, earlier outputs can change; the lab tests that counterexample too.

Question 2. An all-masked row produces NaNs. Diagnose it. Pause now and try your answer. When you are ready, continue. Solution: softmax of all negative infinity is undefined because no allowed value exists. Ensure a valid attention pattern (for example each valid decoder position can see itself), and handle padded query rows explicitly. Do not turn NaNs into arbitrary probabilities and call it fixed.

Question 3. You need a document classifier, not generation. Is a causal decoder mandatory? Pause now and try your answer. When you are ready, continue. Solution: no. A bidirectional encoder can represent complete input context efficiently for classification; compare quality and latency with simpler baselines. For translation or conditional generation, encoder-decoder can be appropriate; for next-token generation, causal decoder is natural. Architecture follows task and evidence.

Question 4. A colleague reads attention weights as “the causal reason.” What would test that interpretation? Pause now and try your answer. When you are ready, continue. Solution: intervene on inputs or components under a controlled analysis, compare alternative paths and evaluate output changes. Even interventions have assumptions; the weight matrix alone is insufficient.

Mastery gate: reconstruct shapes and the first row by hand; run the projection and block tests; explain future-input invariance; then solve the changed-input exercise before reading output. Completing these tasks demonstrates a minimal attention implementation, not mastery of training a full transformer or interpreting a large model.
