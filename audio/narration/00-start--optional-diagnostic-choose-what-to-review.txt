Question 1. Why can fitting a scaler before the training and test split contaminate evaluation? Pause now and try your answer. When you are ready, continue. Solution: test distribution statistics influence the fitted pipeline; fitting belongs within each training fold.

Question 2. Compute the dot product of (1,2) and (3,4). Pause now and try your answer. When you are ready, continue. Solution: 11, a weighted sum, not a distance.

Question 3. A rare fault has prevalence 1%; a detector has 90% sensitivity and 10% false-positive rate. Is a positive result usually correct? Pause now and try your answer. When you are ready, continue. Solution: no; 90 divided by (90 plus 990), about 8.3% precision in 10,000 cases.

Question 4. Differentiate One half of the squared difference between w times x and y with respect to w. Pause now and try your answer. When you are ready, continue. Solution: The quantity w times x minus y, multiplied by x; the x comes from the chain rule.

Question 5. Why is 99% accuracy weak evidence on a 1% positive problem? Pause now and try your answer. When you are ready, continue. Solution: predicting every case negative achieves it.

Question 6. What does a causal attention mask prohibit? Pause now and try your answer. When you are ready, continue. Solution: using later positions while predicting the next token.

Question 7. Why does adding retrieval not guarantee truth? Pause now and try your answer. When you are ready, continue. Solution: retrieval may miss evidence; evidence may be false or outdated; the generator may not follow it.

Question 8. What must a retried payment-like tool call carry? Pause now and try your answer. When you are ready, continue. Solution: an enforced idempotency key and authorization context; a prompt instruction is insufficient.

Question 9. Why is event time alone insufficient for historical feature construction? Pause now and try your answer. When you are ready, continue. Solution: an event may have arrived after the historical decision.

Question 10. What differs between a latency average and a ninety ninth percentile latency? Pause now and try your answer. When you are ready, continue. Solution: mean measures overall average, ninety ninth percentile marks the slow-tail boundary for 99% of observed requests.
