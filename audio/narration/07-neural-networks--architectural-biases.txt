Chapter 7: Neural networks and representation learning. Architectural biases.

Convolutional networks share local filters across spatial positions. This encodes a useful locality and translation bias for images and signals. A 3 by 3 convolution from 3 input channels to 16 output channels has 3 by 3 by 3 by 16 equals 432 weights, plus 16 biases if used. Spatial dimensions and padding alter output size, not this count.

Recurrent networks update a state across sequence steps; long short term memory and GRU gates help manage information flow and gradients. They naturally process variable-length sequences but sequential computation can limit parallel training. Transformers use attention to mix positions; they have different compute and memory tradeoffs. Graph neural networks aggregate neighbor information on graph structures, with risks including oversmoothing, noisy edges and graph leakage across splits.

An autoencoder compresses input to a latent representation then reconstructs it. A variational autoencoder learns a probabilistic latent model using reconstruction and a distribution regularizer. Contrastive learning brings related views closer and separates negatives according to its objective; “negative” may be an accidentally related example, so sampling matters.
