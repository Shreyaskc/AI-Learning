Chapter 12: Multimodal and generative modeling. Three generative families.

This section is an optional orientation to different generation problems. An autoencoder learns to reconstruct an input through a compressed representation. A variational autoencoder introduces a latent probability distribution; training balances reconstruction likelihood with a K L regularizer toward a prior. A generative adversarial network trains a generator against a discriminator; instability and mode collapse can arise when diversity is poorly represented. Neither term is interchangeable with diffusion.

Diffusion training adds noise to data at a chosen step and learns a reverse denoising signal. Let alpha sub bar sub t be the fraction of signal variance retained at step t, and epsilon a standard Gaussian noise sample. A common forward form is The noisy input at step t is the square root of cumulative alpha times the original input, plus the square root of one minus cumulative alpha times noise epsilon, where epsilon is standard Gaussian noise and alpha sub bar sub t describes retained signal. With scalar x0 equals 2, alpha sub bar equals zero point six four and epsilon equals 1, x sub t equals zero point eight by 2 plus zero point six by 1 equals 2.2. If epsilon were known exactly, reconstruction is Two point two minus zero point six, divided by zero point eight, equals two. A learned network estimates noise or related quantities imperfectly.
