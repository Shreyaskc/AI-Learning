Chapter 7: Neural networks and representation learning. Practical extension: train a complete multilayer perceptron and verify every gradient.

The multilayer perceptron in model sub comparison.py uses d equals 2 inputs, h hidden hyperbolic tangent units and one logistic output. W1 has h by d weights, b1 has h biases, v has h output weights and b2 is scalar. Its hidden state is Hidden activation a sub j is the hyperbolic tangent of the sum over k of first-layer weight j k times input k, plus hidden bias j, logit The output score z is the sum of output weight j times hidden activation j, plus output bias b two, and probability sigmoid of z. For h equals 8,d equals 2, there are 16 plus 8 plus 8 plus 1 equals 33 trainable scalars. All are updated; there is no frozen hidden layer.

For one example, let delta equals p minus y. Backprop gives The loss derivative for output weight j equals delta times hidden activation j, The loss derivative for the output bias equals delta, The loss derivative for first-layer weight j k is delta times output weight j, times one minus hidden activation j squared, times input k, and The loss derivative for hidden bias j is delta times output weight j times one minus hidden activation j squared. These follow from the chain rule and derivative of hyperbolic tangent. Batch gradients average over examples; L2 adds lambda times each penalized weight. Biases are deliberately not regularized. The code's flattened parameter layout is documented so you can map every index back to these equations.

Initialization uses seeded small random weights and zero biases. Equal zero weights for all hidden units would make their behavior symmetric and prevent learning diverse features. Tanh is smooth, making numerical gradient checks less ambiguous than rectified linear unit at its kink. It can still saturate when inputs are badly scaled; the train-only standardization is therefore part of the experiment.

The test test every mlp weight and bias gradient uses a two-hidden-unit network and two examples, computes analytic derivatives, then perturbs every parameter by plus or minus 1 times e-5 to obtain centered finite differences. It checks input weights, hidden biases, output weights and output bias, including L2. This is stronger than checking one convenient weight. A correct derivative test still does not prove correct labels, a valid split or convergence on every task.

The chosen width-8 model's regularized training objective decreases from zero point seven zero six four zero one to zero point two one three five three four in 700 full-batch updates. Held-out metrics appear in chapter 05. This is actual training evidence, not simply an imported network returning a tensor. Two endpoint values establish a decrease for this run; they do not replace a detailed learning curve or prove monotonic loss at every update.
