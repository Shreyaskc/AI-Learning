Chapter 12: Multimodal and generative modeling. Representations across modalities.

An encoder turns an input into a representation used by later calculations. For an image, this starts from an array of pixel intensities; audio is a sampled waveform often transformed into time-frequency features. Text is a token sequence. An image encoder may use convolutions or split an image into patches and process patch embeddings with a transformer. An audio pipeline may use frames, spectrograms or learned waveform representations. Sampling rate, resizing, cropping and normalization are part of the input contract.

Contrastive image-text learning brings matching image and caption representations together relative to nonmatching candidates. CLIP is an influential primary example. Consider two normalized image vectors (1,0) and (0,1), with matching text vectors in the same directions. Dot-product score matrix is [[1,0],[0,1]]. At temperature tau equals 1, each correct row probability is e divided by (e plus 1) approximately zero point seven three one; row negative log loss is zero point three one three. Lower tau sharpens this distribution, but shortcut cues and false negatives can still mislead training.

This objective aligns representations under training data; it does not establish complete visual understanding. Captions may omit counts, spatial relations or subtle abnormalities. Evaluate controlled counterexamples: same objects with different counts; left and right swaps; captions differing only in negation; demographic and lighting changes. A global average can hide systematic failures.
