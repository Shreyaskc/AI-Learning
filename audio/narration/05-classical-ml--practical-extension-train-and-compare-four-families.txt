This trains a constant predictor, vector logistic regression, a greedy CART-style classification tree and a small hyperbolic tangent multilayer perceptron. Generate 320 independent examples with seed 31. Features x1 and x2 have different units: x1 is uniform on [negative 1,1], x2 on [negative 100,100]. The target is 1 when exactly one feature is positive, with 3% independent label flips. This XOR-shaped relationship has two disjoint positive quadrants. A single affine score cannot represent it exactly. The label noise deliberately prevents interpreting perfect training fit as the goal.

Freeze 160 training, 80 validation and 80 final-test rows before fitting. The data generator is independent and identically distributed here; this split is not a prescription for time series. Fit feature means and standard deviations on the training rows only, then apply that same transformation to all three sets. The constant predicts training prevalence. Logistic regression minimizes log loss with L2. The tree tests midpoint thresholds and chooses the greatest immediate weighted-Gini improvement, stopping by depth and minimum leaf size. It uses smoothed leaf probabilities The positive count plus one, divided by the leaf count plus two to avoid exact zero and one probabilities on tiny leaves. That smoothing is an explicit design choice affecting log loss.

Train depths 2 and 4 as tree candidates and hidden widths 4 and 8 as multilayer perceptron candidates; select the candidate within each family with lowest validation log loss. Logistic has one fixed configuration. Finally evaluate the selected instances once on the untouched test rows with threshold zero point five. Family tuning budgets differ, so this is an educational comparison, not a fair compute-efficiency benchmark.
