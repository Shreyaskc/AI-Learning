Chapter 9: large language model pretraining, adaptation and alignment. Practical extension: actually train a tiny language model.

Run the Python three language model lab command shown in the written section. It fits a character bigram model, selects smoothing on validation data, evaluates held-out negative log likelihood and samples a sequence. It is a genuine learned probabilistic model, but not a neural network, pretrained large language model or question-answering system.

For each previous character a, training counts how often character b follows it. Add begin and end symbols and an unknown symbol. Estimate The conditional probability of token b given a is the count of that pair plus alpha, divided by the count of a plus alpha times vocabulary size V, where V is the number of predicted symbols and alpha greater than 0 adds smoothing. With observed transitions a leads to b twice and a leads to c once, V equals 4,alpha equals zero point five, the probability of b given a equals (2 plus zero point five) divided by (3 plus 2) equals zero point five, the probability of c given a equals 1.5 divided by 5 equals zero point three, and each of two unseen continuations gets zero point one. The row sums to one. Without smoothing, an unseen test transition would have zero probability and infinite negative log likelihood.

The vocabulary and counts come only from training strings. An unseen held-out character maps to the unknown-token marker; it does not silently expand the trained vocabulary. This trades detail for a well-defined distribution. Evaluation includes the end symbol and reports unknown count. Different unknown policies and tokenization change perplexity, so document them before comparing models.

The corpus consists of three short invented cat and dog sentences repeated eight times. Alpha is selected from {zero point one,zero point five,1} on two validation sentences. The final two sentences include unseen characters. Recorded alpha equals zero point one gives held-out negative log likelihood approximately 1.3990 nats per token and perplexity approximately 4.0513 over 37 tokens including end markers, versus unigram negative log likelihood approximately 2.8748. These tiny, highly related strings do not establish general language capability. The seeded sample cat og og a ma cathemat cand on og can og san. shows local character associations without coherent semantics. Lower perplexity can coexist with nonsense.
