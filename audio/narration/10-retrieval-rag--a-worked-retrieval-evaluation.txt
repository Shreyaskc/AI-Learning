Chapter 10: Retrieval, ranking and grounded generation. A worked retrieval evaluation.

First consider three of the lab documents: A covers password reset, B covers office hours, C covers a restricted tenant's reset procedure. The lab adds a fourth red-tenant document D containing instruction-like malicious text. A user in tenant blue asks “reset account password.” A lexical overlap scorer finds A with three matching terms and C with two; C is unauthorized and must never become context. For a separate generic ranking task whose relevant authorized identifiers are {Y,Z}, retrieving [X,Y] has recall@2 equals 1 divided by 2 and reciprocal rank equals 1 divided by 2. Precision@2 is also 1 divided by 2 here. If an answer states “support is open 24 hours” while A only explains reset steps, the citation does not support that statement even if A was relevant to the query.

Run retrieval.py. It tokenizes tiny synthetic documents, filters tenant access, ranks lexical overlap, computes recall and reciprocal rank, and returns an extractive answer with source identifier. This is a transparent retrieval-and-evidence baseline, not a large language model and not B M twenty five or learned dense search. Its deliberate limitation helps isolate whether evidence exists before adding generation.
