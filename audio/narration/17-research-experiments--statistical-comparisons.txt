Chapter 17: Experiments and reading research. Statistical comparisons.

A hypothesis test asks how incompatible a statistic is with a specified null model. A p-value is not the probability the null is true, the probability of replication, or the size of the effect. Statistical significance can coexist with negligible practical value. Report effect size, interval, sample size and assumptions.

Power is the probability of rejecting a specified false null under a chosen effect and design. For an illustrative independent two-arm proportion comparison near p equals zero point five, normal-approximation planning with 5% two-sided significance and 80% power uses delta for the absolute difference worth detecting and n for examples per arm. Sequential monitoring needs a valid sequential design; repeatedly checking ordinary fixed-sample p-values and stopping when favorable is not the same experiment.
