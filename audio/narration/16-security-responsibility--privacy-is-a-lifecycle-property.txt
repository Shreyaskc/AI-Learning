Chapter 16: Security, privacy and responsible A.I. Privacy is a lifecycle property.

Minimize collection, classify sensitivity, restrict purpose, define retention, encrypt transport and storage and audit access. De-identification is not a blanket anonymity guarantee; combinations of quasi-identifiers can reidentify people. Training can memorize rare sensitive strings. Decide whether private data may leave a boundary before building an integration, and use verified contracts and policies rather than assumptions about a provider.

The next two privacy methods are optional recognition-level material, not controls implemented by these labs. Differential privacy limits how much an output distribution can change when one person's data changes, under a formal neighboring-dataset definition. Parameters epsilon and delta describe the guarantee, not a simple percent-privacy score. Composition accumulates privacy loss; noise, clipping and accounting affect utility. This course introduces the concept only; applying it requires dedicated implementation and accounting expertise. Federated learning keeps some raw data distributed, but gradients and updates can still leak information without additional protections.

Deletion must cover raw sources, derived features, vector indexes, caches, memories and documented backup retention. Updating a retrieval store is different from removing influence from trained weights; machine unlearning remains technically and empirically demanding. Avoid promising immediate exact erasure from weights without a validated method.
