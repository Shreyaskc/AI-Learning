If two input columns are identical, the linear score depends on their coefficient sum. For x1 equals x2, coefficients (1,0) and (0,1) produce the same predictions. Individual coefficients are not identifiable from this design alone. L2 can select a minimum-norm convention, but that does not reveal which feature caused an outcome. Near-duplicate columns can make coefficient estimates unstable even when predictions look stable. Audit units, rank, regularization and interpretation separately.

K-means is not the only definition of a cluster. DBSCAN groups points connected through sufficiently dense neighborhoods, using a radius epsilon and minimum-neighbor count. With points at 0,zero point one,zero point two,5 on a line, radius zero point one five and minimum count 2 including the point itself, the first three connect through neighborhoods while 5 is isolated. This can capture non-spherical structure and identify noise, but results depend strongly on scaling, density variation and epsilon. This lesson explains the mechanism; it does not claim an executed DBSCAN implementation.

In high dimensions, many irrelevant coordinates can dominate distance, and local neighborhoods become sparse at a fixed sample size. A nearest-neighbor method may need more data, better representations or task-informed metric choices. Adding dimensions is not automatically adding useful information. Two-dimensional visualizations such as t S N E and U map can distort global distances and vary with settings; separated blobs do not establish real-world categories or causal subpopulations. Confirm conclusions with original-space evaluation and task evidence.
