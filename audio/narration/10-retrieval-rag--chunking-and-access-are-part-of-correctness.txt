Chapter 10: Retrieval, ranking and grounded generation. Chunking and access are part of correctness.

Chunking divides sources into units that fit retrieval and context budgets. Preserve headings, tables and identifiers; arbitrary character cuts may separate a claim from its qualification. Overlap can recover boundaries but increases duplicate tokens and index size. Store source identifier, version, offsets, timestamp, ownership, tenant and access policy alongside each chunk.

Enforce authorization in trusted application and database logic before content enters a prompt. Post-filtering a small candidate list can reduce authorized recall; measure this under realistic permission distributions. Never rely on a large language model to decide which private documents a user may see. Cache keys must include authorization scope and source and model versions. A deleted source should disappear from indexes, caches, summaries and derived memory according to a documented lifecycle.
