Chapter 14: Training compute, hardware and inference serving. Training memory and parallelism.

This deeper section switches from serving a selected model to training its parameters. A model with P parameters may need weights, gradients, optimizer states, activations and temporary buffers during training. A rough mixed-precision Adam accounting sometimes uses 2 bytes for weights, 2 for gradients, 4 for master weights and 8 for two FP32 moments: 16P bytes before activations. Actual frameworks and configurations differ; state assumptions rather than quoting 16 as universal. At P equals 1 billion this subtotal is 16 gigabytes in decimal units, plus potentially substantial activations.

Data parallelism replicates a model over workers, each processing different examples, then combines gradients. Tensor parallelism splits operations or tensors across workers and requires layer-level communication. Pipeline parallelism places groups of layers on different workers and passes activations, with scheduling bubbles. Sharded data parallelism partitions states across workers; ZeRO describes staged partitioning of optimizer state, gradients and parameters. Adding graphics processors can become limited by collectives, network bandwidth, synchronization, imbalance or input pipelines.

An all-reduce combines tensors across workers and returns the result to each. A ring all-reduce of S bytes per worker over N workers moves roughly Two times N minus one, times S, divided by N bytes per worker in the idealized bandwidth accounting; latency and topology add cost. For N equals 4,S equals 1 gigabytes, that is 1.5 gigabytes per worker, not zero overhead. Gradient accumulation can raise effective batch without storing all examples at once, but changes update frequency and may affect optimization.

Checkpointing saves recoverable model and optimizer and data-progress state. Activation checkpointing instead recomputes some intermediates during backprop to save activation memory. They solve different problems. Reproducibility also needs RNG and sampler state; distributed failure recovery must avoid skipped or duplicated data beyond the declared policy.
